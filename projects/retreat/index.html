Retreat into Your Sound

projects

Through an AI-native streaming app,
remember what you truly love.

A quiet listening room, reached from any screen. Something in your library has always known. Not lost — only buried, beneath the noise of the feed. This is not another catalogue app. It is a return — to taste. Nothing generic is served here. Only what has always lived in your history is resurfaced. In the rhythm of the interface, the ear finds what last week's charts forgot. Not outward — but within.

aira

The sound library

साउंड लाइब्रेरी

This is not merely a player, nor is it intended as a background jukebox. Within the app's dark-red glass interface, the library is carried as the core of the product — 41,234 tracks, uploads in MP3, WAV and FLAC, organized so the collection you built over years stays yours.

The design does not push you outward toward charts. It brings you inward — deeper into your own listening, more precisely into mood. It works on the home feed, the explore grid, and the made-for-you shelf — gently surfacing what no longer gets played, and restoring what has always been there.

Not a chart handed down from outside. But a taste quietly remembered from within.

medicine

The Journey

सुनने की यात्रा

01

Preparation

तैयारी

The journey begins at sign-up. Through a short taste profile — artists, languages, decades — we map the themes present in your listening and clarify the intention you bring to the app. With careful attention to your library and history, Aira prepares your first mix — so the experience opens tuned, and with clarity.

02

In the Flow

प्रवाह

In a dark glass interface with a warm red glow, the session begins under the guidance of the home feed and the AI mix builder. Releasing the skip-hunt, you return to full albums and long playlists. What has long sat unplayed begins to gently resurface. There is no need to force discovery. In listening, your original taste naturally begins to emerge.

03

Integration

एकीकरण

The experience does not end with the session. In many ways, it is only the beginning. Through karaoke mode and lyric view with live translation, what has been heard is woven back into everyday life — in the commute, in the kitchen, in the way one chooses to sing.

04

Transformation

रूपांतरण

What arises from this process is not a temporary hit of novelty. It is a quiet, grounded shift — emerging through the act of curating. Choosing from taste, not trend. Queues that assemble with ease. A deep trust in your own ear. As one returns to their natural rotation, the library itself begins to realign. Aira walks alongside you — every session of the way.

Abhinav｜Listener · Premium, 2026

This was my first AI-built mix. Yet I was able to press play without doubt or skipping, because I had deep trust in the library.

Even now, when I look back, the search, the translation, and the flow of that first queue were all quietly right. It remains one of the best product experiences I have had, and the playlist is still in my rotation.

It truly felt like getting my library back—returning to the songs I was raised on. Right after the first session, the mixes on my home screen, the covers in the explore grid, and even the little lyric line under the scrubber all felt considered. I remember feeling genuinely glad that an app respected what I had already collected.

As weeks have passed, I have kept digging deeper into the catalogue. I have also begun to notice the small touches that appear in daily use — how karaoke mode ducks the vocals, how the translation follows the beat. I can feel that the design is still paying off.

For those who will open Aira in the future, I believe their first evening with the mix builder will be worth more than any review. My thanks to the team, and to whoever taught the search to read half-remembered lyrics.

Tejas · Indie artist · on Aira

I was an unexplored artist. Until the app recommended me, nobody searched my name. I believed that discovery was a budget line, and that not charting or not being playlisted was something to be ashamed of. But when Music Ai answered a listener’s question with my song—when plays actually arrived—it was surreal. And I realized that I hadn’t even known this side of the platform.

The made-for-you shelf, and the search, showed me. I felt an incredible sense of visibility, as if my catalogue had been laid out on a table. There is no such thing as an audience-less artist. We are all unfound—and that is fixable. It’s okay to be small. It’s okay not to have marketing.

Since the feature went live, I’ve watched my uploads, my saves, and even the way I title my songs changing at an incredible speed. Listeners came back to me. Right up until then, I was chasing playlist placements. But Aira wasn’t something that requires chasing. The library surfaces everything. It gave my songs a shelf.

From here on, in my releases, it’s about the songs I make and how I choose to share them. And when I think about that, I feel an overwhelming excitement for the next upload. I never knew that publishing music could feel this direct, this within reach.

This platform is generous. We are able to be found. We have a shelf of our own. And we are unfinished—and that too is fine. To the library, to the team, to the listeners who pressed play—and to everything that queues here and now—I send my thanks.

Mira ｜Archivist · Uploads, FLAC

I wanted an app that keeps my uploads and my streams in one place. It may sound like something obvious, but nothing was able to do that.

For my rips, for my playlists—whenever I switched apps, I would naturally lose one library or the other. Even though I wanted to merge them, I couldn’t. In the midst of that clutter, I chose to move everything into Aira.

When the import began and I first saw my whole listening life in one place, it was only for a moment, but honestly, I was overwhelmed. Even so, the app kept every playlist intact and every file findable, which gave me a deep sense of reassurance.

I could finally see my rips and my streams side by side, and that completeness meant everything. With the uploads library, the old mess gradually transformed into a collection filled with order. I simply played what was there, received it as it was—college recordings and all.

I was getting something I truly needed. The order that existed there was something I could simply accept, and it brought a sense of familiarity, as if I was rediscovering albums I had always owned. It felt like I could enjoy it simply by pressing shuffle.

At the same time, I realized—not just intellectually, but through daily use—that I had been tolerating fragmentation for years. I came to feel, “My collection has always been whole. I am someone who keeps everything.”

Even after the migration ended, when the app quietly suggested a playlist built from my own uploads, I actually smiled at my phone. I felt so full—grateful that the archive survived, and that I could finally stop maintaining three apps. Even after returning to daily listening, that feeling has continued to take root, day by day. Now, the choice to play my own collection arises naturally, and I am able to gently choose it.
